## 0. Read this first — what I could and could not measure

**I was not able to read your PageSpeed Insights scores.** The two report URLs you sent are
client-rendered single-page apps; fetching them returns the page shell, not the Lighthouse
result. I am not going to quote scores I did not see.

What I *did* retrieve from that fetch is one real, load-bearing finding:

> **Your page has no CrUX field data.** Google's own words on the report: the Chrome User
> Experience Report does not have sufficient real-world speed data for this page.

That matters more than any lab score. It means:

* Your **Core Web Vitals assessment in Search Console is running on nothing.** Google has no
  real-user signal for this URL, so the CWV component of page experience is not being
  computed from your actual visitors.
* Every number in a PSI run today is **lab-simulated** — a Moto G-class CPU on a throttled
  1.6 Mbps link. Useful as a diagnostic, but it is not what your visitors experience.
* This is a **traffic-volume** problem, not a code problem. CrUX needs a minimum number of
  eligible samples. The fix is visitors, not markup.

Everything else below was measured directly from the repository you sent and from a live
fetch of the deployed page (which I confirmed matches the repo byte-for-byte in structure).
Where I could not verify something, I say so.

## 2. Findings

Severity: **S1** = actively costing money or breaking · **S2** = measurable degradation ·
**S3** = hygiene / hardening.

### S1 — Fourteen live redirects pointed at anchors that do not exist

`_redirects` mapped 22 rules to `/#light`, `/#measure` and `/#close`. **None of those ids are
on the page.** The real section ids are `#work`, `#services`, `#package`, `#guarantee`,
`#contact`, `#virtual-staging`, `#top`.

Every visitor arriving from an indexed WordPress URL — `/video-drone-media`, `/floor-plans`,
`/drone`, `/aerial`, `/contact`, `/services` and eleven more — was 301'd to the homepage and
dropped at the top with a dead fragment. This is inherited link equity landing on the wrong
content. It is the single highest-value defect in this audit and it is invisible in
Lighthouse, which only ever tests the URL you hand it.

**Fixed.** Rewritten to 49 rules, and I added an automated check that asserts every `/#`
target resolves to a real `id` in `index.html`. It currently passes with zero dead anchors.

### S1 — Scroll handler forces synchronous layout on every tick

`main.js` bound `sweep()` directly to `scroll` with no throttle. `sweep()` called
`getBoundingClientRect()` on every `.reveal` element — around 50 of them — plus every counter,
on **every scroll event, for the entire life of the page**, including long after all of them
had already been revealed. Each `getBoundingClientRect()` after a style mutation forces a
synchronous layout.

On the low-end device Lighthouse emulates this is a direct hit to Total Blocking Time and to
INP, and on a real phone it is the difference between a smooth scroll and a sticky one.

**Fixed.** All scroll-driven work now shares a single `requestAnimationFrame` batch. Jobs
retire themselves when finished and the `scroll` listener is removed once the queue empties.
The `IntersectionObserver` path — which is what virtually every real browser takes — no longer
has a redundant scroll fallback running alongside it at all.

### S2 — The paper-grain texture re-blended the entire viewport every frame

`body::after` was `position: fixed; inset: 0; mix-blend-mode: multiply` at 5% opacity — a
full-screen blended compositing layer, permanently. Blend modes cannot be handled by a simple
composited layer copy; the GPU must re-blend the covered area whenever anything beneath it
moves.

**Fixed.** Blend mode removed, opacity nudged 0.05 → 0.055 to compensate. Over warm
near-white surfaces at that opacity, normal alpha compositing is visually indistinguishable
and costs nothing per frame. The texture is unchanged to the eye.

### S2 — A 52-second infinite animation ran for the whole session

The trust marquee's `animation: marquee-flow 52s linear infinite` never stopped, even though
the strip is off screen for the overwhelming majority of any visit. That keeps a compositor
layer alive and burns battery for nothing.

**Fixed.** An `IntersectionObserver` toggles `animation-play-state` — it runs when visible and
parks when not. Zero visual change. The existing `prefers-reduced-motion` handling was already
correct and is untouched.

### S2 — LCP image was 2.4× larger than it needed to be

The mobile hero — your Largest Contentful Paint element — was a 960 × 1200 WebP at **124.5 KB**.
On Lighthouse's throttled 1.6 Mbps mobile profile that is roughly **620 ms of transfer alone**,
before decode, on the critical path.

Two problems compounded: no AVIF anywhere on the site, and 960 px is over-served for the
360–412 px viewports that dominate mobile traffic.

**Fixed.** Added an 800 w step (exact for a 393 px viewport at DPR 2) and AVIF encodes throughout:

| | before | after | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mobile LCP image** | 124.5 KB | **51.7 KB** | **−58 %** |
| All content images | 4,724 KB | **2,566 KB** | **−45.7 %** (2.11 MB) |

60 AVIF variants generated at CRF 32. The `<link rel=preload>` now carries
`type="image/avif"`, so browsers without AVIF support never fetch a file they cannot decode
and fall through cleanly to the existing WebP.

### S2 — Font swap had no metric-matched fallback

All ten faces used `font-display: swap` with no size adjustment. Between first paint and font
arrival, text was laid out in Georgia/Arial at completely different metrics, then re-laid-out
when Cormorant and Jost landed — a layout shift on the hero headline in the exact window CLS
is sampled.

**Fixed.** I measured the shipped `.woff2` files directly with FreeType:

| face | ascent | descent | avg lowercase advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cormorant Garamond | 0.9240 em | 0.2870 em | 0.4319 em |
| Jost | 1.0700 em | 0.3750 em | 0.4599 em |

and derived matched fallback faces (Cormorant → Georgia at `size-adjust: 80.99%`,
`ascent-override: 114.09%`; Jost → Arial at `87.12%` / `122.82%`).

**Honest caveat:** this technique needs a locally-installed font to adjust. On most Android
devices neither Georgia nor Arial is present, the `@font-face` is simply invalid, and the
stack falls through to generic serif/sans exactly as it does today. So this is a **desktop
win and an Android no-op** — strictly better, never worse. Worth verifying visually on a
desktop first load with the cache cleared.

### S2 — Fonts shipped that never render

`Playfair Display` (3 files) and `Marcellus` (1 file) were declared and deployed. Marcellus is
referenced nowhere. Playfair appears only as the *second* entry in the `--serif` stack — and
because Cormorant is self-hosted and always available, Playfair can never win. Browsers do not
download unused `@font-face` files, so this cost **nothing at runtime** — but it was 106 KB of
dead deploy weight and four misleading declarations.

**Fixed.** Removed. Font payload 322 KB → 216 KB.

### S2 — Cache-busting version strings had drifted apart

`index.html` requested `styles.min.css?v=20260727a`. `404.html` and `thank-you.html` requested
`?v=20260717c`. Under your `/assets/* immutable, max-age=31536000` policy those are two
separate year-long cache entries for a byte-identical file.

**Fixed.** All pages now on `?v=20260730`.

### S2 — `robots.txt` and `noindex` cancelled each other out

`thank-you.html` carried both `Disallow:` in robots.txt *and* `<meta name="robots"
content="noindex">`. A crawler blocked from fetching a page can never read the noindex inside
it. The two directives are mutually defeating.

**Fixed.** `Disallow` lines removed so Google can fetch and obey the `noindex`.

*Corrected 2026-07-30 on review:* the original text of this section claimed
`design-system.html` "was publicly reachable with no robots directive at all" and now carries
`noindex, nofollow`. Both halves were wrong. That page already carried
`<meta name="robots" content="noindex">` on line 6 before this pass, and it still carries
exactly that — `nofollow` was never added and the file was not modified. Removing its
`Disallow` line is still the right call for the reason given above, but nothing about the page
itself changed.

### S2 — Accessibility gaps in the two modal surfaces

* **Lightbox:** declared `aria-modal="true"` with no focus trap. Tab moved focus behind the
  open dialog into the page.
* **Mobile nav:** no Escape key, no focus trap, and rotating past the 881 px breakpoint while
  open left a hidden drawer holding `body { overflow: hidden }` — a scroll-locked page with no
  visible way out.
* **Before/after sliders:** the `<input type="range">` is `opacity: 0` by design, so its
  `:focus-visible` outline was invisible too. Keyboard users had no idea the control was focused.
* **Showreel:** the play button is removed on click, destroying the focused element and
  dumping focus to `<body>`.

**All fixed** — shared focus trap, `inert` on the background (which removes it from the
accessibility tree in a way `aria-modal` alone does not reliably do), Escape handling,
breakpoint recovery, a focus ring surfaced on the slider handle via `:has()`, and focus moved
to the `<video>` when the play button disappears.

### S3 — `<img class="lightbox-img" src="">`

An empty `src` attribute is invalid and causes some browsers to re-request the current page
URL. Removed; intrinsic `1200 × 800` added (every gallery source is that size), so the viewer
no longer shifts as the image lands.

### S3 — Other hygiene

* `text-rendering: optimizeLegibility` on `body` — forces kerning and ligature shaping across
  the whole document. Removed.
* `--charcoal-soft` used via `var()` but never defined. Defined.
* `will-change: transform` sat permanently on the 2000 px drone-band image. Now applied only
  while the band is actually being parallaxed.
* Two double-encoded em-dashes (`â€”`) in CSS comments — harmless at runtime, but a signal of
  encoding drift. Repaired.
* Added `frame-ancestors 'self'`, `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy`, `X-DNS-Prefetch-Control`,
  `theme-color`, `og:image:alt`, `color-scheme: light only`, and `overscroll-behavior: contain`
  on the lightbox.

## 3. Things I checked that turned out to be **fine**

I want these on the record so nobody re-litigates them later:

* **The MP4 is faststart.** I parsed the container atoms by hand — `moov` sits at offset 32,
  before `mdat`. Playback can begin without buffering the whole file. This is the single most
  common video defect and you do not have it.
* **Contrast passes.** All twelve token pairs meet WCAG AA. `--muted` at 5.28:1, `--gold-deep`
  at 5.42:1 on paper. `--gold` measures 2.37:1 but is only ever used on decorative stars (which
  carry an `sr-only` text equivalent) and a 12 %-opacity watermark — no text depends on it.
  Someone already did this work properly.
* **Every image already had `width`, `height`, `loading` and `decoding`.** That is the usual
  source of CLS and it was already handled.
* **GA was already deferred correctly** off the critical path via `requestIdleCallback` with an
  interaction fallback. Genuinely good work — I only propagated it to the two secondary pages.
* **The lightbox uses `visibility: hidden` when closed**, so its buttons are correctly out of
  the tab order. No `aria-hidden-focus` violation.
* **The showreel already loaded nothing until clicked.**

## 4. The video: an honest negative result

I tried to shrink the 19.9 MB showreel and **failed to do so usefully.**

* 1080p H.264 at CRF 23 came out at **34 MB — larger than your original.**
* 720p at CRF 27 reached 15.4 MB — only 26 % smaller, with visible quality loss.

Your existing encode is 1920×1080 / 25 fps / 71.7 s at 2.2 Mbps. That is already efficient.
This is high-entropy drone footage with grain and fast motion; there is no local re-encode
that meaningfully wins. I deleted both attempts rather than ship a degraded duplicate.

**The right fix is architectural, not a better encode:** adaptive-bitrate delivery.
Cloudflare Stream, Mux, or a click-to-load YouTube facade pointed at the channel you already
run (`youtube.com/@sixthvision86`). That gives per-device bitrate ladders and a global CDN.

**But be clear about what this is and is not:** because the video already loads nothing until
clicked, it costs you **zero Lighthouse points today**. This is a mobile-data-burn and
bandwidth-bill problem, not a score problem. Do not let anyone sell it to you as a performance
emergency.

## 5. What I deliberately did **not** do

* **No critical-CSS inlining.** Your stylesheet is 38.5 KB raw / ~8 KB compressed, same-origin,
  over HTTP/3. Inlining would save roughly one round trip and permanently introduce a
  duplication that drifts out of sync. Not worth the maintenance debt at this size.
* **No `aggregateRating` in the schema**, despite the page displaying "Rated 5.0 on Google."
  Google explicitly disregards self-serving review markup for `LocalBusiness` — markup where the
  business supplies ratings about itself. Adding it invites a manual action and yields no rich
  result. I added `makesOffer` (the real $650 package) and `VideoObject` (the showreel) instead,
  both of which are legitimate and eligible.
* **No `FAQPage` schema.** Google restricted FAQ rich results to authoritative government and
  health sites in 2023. It would be pure markup weight.
* **No hashed CSP.** I computed the SHA-256 of your inline GA bootstrap and left it as a
  *commented* line in `_headers` with an explanation. Applying it live would mean that editing
  that inline block by a single byte silently blocks the script and kills analytics with no
  visible symptom. That is a booby trap without a build step to recompute the hash. Your call,
  documented rather than sprung on you.
* **No AVIF for the agency logos.** They carry alpha, and the `ffmpeg`/libaom path available to
  me drops the alpha channel — I verified this, the output came back opaque. Two of the nine
  (`hodges`, `loveco`) are already RGB and rely on `mix-blend-mode: multiply` to hide their
  white backgrounds. Converting them needs `avifenc` or `cavif` on the build machine. They total
  180 KB and are lazy-loaded, so this is low priority.

## 9. Independent review before push — three shipped defects, and one undocumented win

The bundle above was reviewed against a fresh clone and driven in a real browser before it was
committed. Section 8 claimed 33 fixes; three of them were not actually delivered by the shipped
files, and one improvement was delivered without being written down. All measurements below were
taken from the DOM and the Resource Timing API, not inferred.

### 9.1 The lightbox disabled itself (functional, ship-blocking)

`background()` set `inert` on `#main`, but `#lightbox` was a *child* of `#main`, so opening the
dialog inerted the dialog. Measured with it open: `btnClose.focus()` left `document.activeElement`
on `BODY`, and a hit-test at the close button's own centre returned the header behind it. `body`
kept `overflow: hidden`, and the only exit was the Escape key — i.e. no exit at all on touch.

Three separate causes, all fixed:

* The dialog now sits as a **sibling** of header/main/footer instead of inside `main`.
* `inert` is applied to **every body-level sibling** rather than a hand-listed `main` + footer.
  That list was also incomplete: the skip link, `.topbar` and `.wa-float` all live at body level,
  so six elements are inerted, not two. Only what was set is cleared on close.
* `.lightbox` transitioned `visibility` over `.4s`. `visibility` is a **discrete** property, so it
  flipped at the 50% mark and the dialog stayed `hidden` for the first 200 ms — long enough that
  focus and hit-testing failed even once it was out of `main`. It now flips at `0s` on open and is
  deferred `0s .4s` on close, so the fade-out keeps its full 400 ms.

Verified after the fix: focus lands on the close button, all three controls hit-test to
themselves, nothing behind the dialog is focusable, Escape closes, `inert` is fully cleared, and
focus returns to the trigger.

### 9.2 The AVIF encodes were not wired up

21 of 28 `<source type="image/avif">` elements carried srcsets containing only `.webp` files. The
`type` attribute advertised AVIF, the browser selected the source, and then fetched a WebP — so
**only 10 of the 62 shipped AVIF files were reachable at all** and the "−45.7 % on content images"
claim in §2 was not being realised. The remaining 52 files were dead weight in the deploy.

Fixed: every AVIF-typed source now points at its real `.avif`, each substitution checked against
the file on disk. Reachable AVIF went from 10 files / 753 KB to **50 files / 2,078 KB**. Confirmed
in-browser: 26 rendered images now resolve to `.avif`. The 18 that do not are the nine agency
logos (counted twice for the marquee duplicate) — exactly the set §5 excluded for alpha reasons.

### 9.3 The hero was an LCP regression, not a −58 % win

The hero markup had a `<picture>` **nested inside** a `<picture>`. The selection algorithm only
consults sources that are siblings of the `<img>`, so the inner element's single source won and
the outer element's three — including both `media="(max-width: 700px)"` mobile rules — were dead.

| at 375 px / DPR 2 | rendered | preload |
|---|---|---|
| live before | `hero-placeholder-960.webp` — 124 KB | 124 KB, used |
| bundle as shipped | `hero-placeholder-1600.webp` — 177 KB | 51 KB AVIF, **discarded** |
| after this fix | `hero-placeholder-800.avif` — **51 KB** | same file, used |

So the shipped bundle put **228 KB** on the mobile LCP path where live used 124 KB. Un-nesting the
element delivers the −58 % the audit claimed. Desktop likewise now renders `2400.avif` (183 KB)
in place of `2400.webp` (341 KB).

### 9.4 Cache-bust had to move

Both minified assets changed here, but §2 left every page on `?v=20260730` — the same token the
*previous* CSS and JS were served under. With `/assets/* max-age=31536000, immutable`, returning
visitors would have kept the old files for a year and never received the lightbox fix. All pages
are now on **`?v=20260730b`**. `_headers` already warns about this; it was simply missed.

### 9.5 Undocumented win: the italic serif face was never declared

Not in the changelog, but real and worth recording. `assets/fonts/cormorantgaramond-400-italic.woff2`
has been shipping in the repo with **no `@font-face` rule referencing it** — zero references in the
live stylesheet. Every italic serif on the site therefore fell past Cormorant, past the
never-installed Playfair Display, and rendered in **Georgia italic at full size**, roughly 27 %
wider than the intended face. That covers `.hero h1 em` and the testimonial pull-quotes.

This pass added the missing declaration, so italic serif now renders in real Cormorant Garamond
italic. That is the entire explanation for the one layout change in the diff: the testimonials
section is 71 px shorter (a quote wraps one line fewer) and the page is 70 px shorter overall.
Every other section measured pixel-identical to live. Nothing is clipped.

### 9.6 Still-unreachable AVIFs, on purpose

12 of the 62 remain unreferenced, and should stay: `hero-twilight-*` (4) are the documented
alternate-hero swap set, and `gal-hall-*`, `gal-twilight-*`, `intro-editorial-*`,
`card-twilight-1200`, `footer-texture` are parity encodes whose `.webp` twins are themselves
already unused (`footer-texture.webp` survives only inside a CSS comment as a revert note). They
match existing repo practice rather than adding a new category of dead file.

### 9.7 Pre-existing, unchanged

At DPR 2 the desktop hero preload fetches **both** `imagesrcset` candidates and uses one. The
pristine live build does exactly the same thing with its WebP pair, so this is not a regression
and was left alone — but the wasted candidate is now 110 KB of AVIF rather than 177 KB of WebP.
Worth a separate look; changing it means choosing a single file and trading off DPR-1 against
DPR-2 visitors.

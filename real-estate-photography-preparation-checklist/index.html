The real estate photography preparation checklist.

The easiest way to improve a photo shoot is to solve the preventable distractions before the camera arrives. This checklist is built from Sixth Vision's operating experience across more than 50,000 photographed properties — not from generic staging theory.

Before photo day

Prepare the frame, not just the room.

Photographers can correct exposure, verticals and colour. They cannot make a pile of everyday clutter disappear from every angle without changing what the property actually looks like. The goal is a calm, truthful frame where the eye goes to space, light and selling features first.

Do the practical work the day before. On shoot day, the property should need only a final five-minute walk-through — not a last-minute clean, furniture move or car shuffle.

Room-by-room

The working checklist.

Whole home

  • Declutter visible surfaces and floors
  • Hide cords, chargers, remotes and small appliances
  • Remove personal paperwork, medications and valuables
  • Clean windows, mirrors and glossy surfaces
  • Replace blown bulbs; keep bulb colour consistent
  • Open blinds and curtains unless privacy requires otherwise
  • Move pet beds, bowls, toys and litter trays out of frame

Kitchen

  • Clear benches of dish racks, detergents and everyday appliances
  • Remove magnets, papers and photos from the fridge
  • Clean cooktop, splashback and rangehood
  • Hide bins, tea towels and cleaning products
  • Keep styling simple: one considered object beats ten small ones

Living & dining

  • Straighten cushions, rugs and dining chairs
  • Remove excess furniture if it narrows circulation
  • Hide remote controls, cables and children's clutter
  • Keep coffee and dining tables visually quiet
  • Turn on lamps that are part of the room's design

Bedrooms

  • Make beds tightly and smooth the linen
  • Clear bedside tables except for one or two deliberate items
  • Remove laundry, bags, exercise equipment and visible storage
  • Keep wardrobes closed unless they are a selling feature
  • Check that lamps and overhead lights work

Bathrooms & laundry

  • Hide toiletries, toothbrushes, cleaning products and bins
  • Close toilet lids
  • Use clean, matching towels
  • Dry shower glass, mirrors and taps so streaks do not catch light
  • Clear laundry benches and remove baskets from view

Exterior & outdoor

  • Move cars away from the façade and driveway if possible
  • Hide bins, hoses, tools and garden equipment
  • Mow lawns, trim edges and sweep paved areas
  • Remove pool cleaners, covers and loose toys when practical
  • Set outdoor furniture neatly and clean the barbecue if visible
  • Check exterior lights for dusk or twilight shoots

When extra media is booked

Photography prep is only half the brief.

Floor plans

Keep access clear around walls and through every room. Garages, storage rooms and large sheds may need to be entered and measured, so unlock them before the appointment.

Video & reels

Video sees more than a still frame because the camera moves. Clear hallways, benchtops and transition spaces, and remove anything you would not want visible during a continuous walk-through.

Drone

Move vehicles, bins and temporary clutter from the driveway and yard where practical. Let Sixth Vision know about access constraints, nearby works or anything unusual that could affect the aerial brief.

The final five minutes

One last lap before the photographer arrives.

Walk from the street to the back fence as though you are seeing the property for the first time. Look for the things the eye catches immediately: a car in the hero angle, a bin beside the front door, a crooked cushion, a bathmat on the floor, a charging cable on a kitchen bench, a light that is a different colour from the rest.

If the property is occupied, choose one temporary storage area that will not be photographed early — usually the garage or a single closed room — rather than moving clutter from room to room during the shoot.
